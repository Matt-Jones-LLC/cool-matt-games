Downhill

Click anywhere on the map to drop a marble. Each one only knows which way is downhill from where it sits, and it keeps stepping that way. That is gradient descent, the method used to train neural networks.

DownhillNo marbles yet. Click the map.

Darker means lower. The ring marks the lowest point on the map. A marble that stops anywhere else has found a valley, but not the deepest one.

What this has to do with AI

Training a model means adjusting millions of numbers to make its error smaller. Think of the error as height on a landscape. Training walks downhill on it, one small step at a time, exactly as these marbles do, only in far more than two dimensions.

Step size

Called the learning rate. Small steps are safe and slow. Big steps cover ground fast, and past a point the marble overshoots the valley floor, bounces between the walls and can fly off the map. Turn it up and watch.

Momentum

With momentum a marble keeps some of its speed from the last step, like a real ball. That lets it roll through shallow dips that would trap a marble with none. Try the same map at 0 and at 0.9 and compare how many reach the ring.
